Problem

SMEs die on cash flow. They ship goods, issue an invoice with 30–90 day terms, then wait months for money they need now to make payroll and buy materials. Factoring fixes this — a financier advances cash against the invoice and collects from the buyer later — but underwriting (should we advance? how much? at what discount?) is slow, manual, and closed to small suppliers. There is no rail to do it programmatically in stablecoins.

Description

Letta is an AI agent that is a credit committee with money at stake. It reads the buyer's real on-chain payment history on Arc, prices an advance (advance % + discount rate) with a written reasoning memo, disburses USDC to the supplier now, and runs a 3-way repayment waterfall as the buyer pays — recovering the factor's advance + fee first, surplus to the supplier. Partial and late payments are first-class: the agent re-assesses exposure live on every repayment.

The autonomy is genuine because the decision is a judgment with real money at risk, not "approve a document I wrote" — the failure mode that makes most agentic-finance demos read as theater. Built on Arc: native-USDC settlement, sub-second finality. It reuses ArcPay's PayPerCall Paid events as the buyer's unfakeable credit signal, and Obol's decide→pay→verify agent loop, repointed from "which service to buy" to "should I advance against this invoice and at what price."

Products Used (Circle / Arc)

  • USDC — native gas, every payout is pay{value}; no ERC-20 approval dance.
  • Arc smart contracts — FactoringPool.sol deployed on Arc testnet; the buyer's credit signal is on-chain Paid receipts from ArcPay's PayPerCall.
  • Arc sub-second finality — fund, partial, and settle all clear in under half a second.
  • Anthropic Claude — the underwriter.
  • Roadmap: Circle Wallets (agent custody), Circle Gateway + CCTP (cross-chain advance capital), USYC (idle-float yield as the factor's funding source).

Architecture

  invoice (JSON) + buyer address
            |
  +---------v---------------------------+
  |   UNDERWRITING AGENT (Claude)       |  <- reuses Obol's decide->pay->verify loop
  |   read on-chain history -> score    |
  |   risk -> price advance% + discount  |
  |   -> memo -> re-underwrite on partial |
  +---+-----------------------+---------+
 reads | Paid history   fund / | repay / waterfall
       v                       v
 +-------------+      +----------------------+
 | Arc Paid    |      |  FactoringPool.sol    |  (deployed,
 | history     |      |  Arc testnet          |   Arc testnet)
 | (PayPerCall)|      |  push+escrow payouts  |
 +-------------+      +----------------------+

Push-with-escrow-fallback payouts: a griefing recipient can never brick the waterfall or strand the factor's recovery (a 5-lens adversarial security review found + fixed a real push-payment DoS and an overpayment leak before deploy).

Product Feedback (Circle / Arc)

What worked: Arc's native-USDC gas made the contract trivial — payouts are just pay{value}, no token approvals. Sub-second finality made fund → partial → settle feel instantaneous, which is what sells an agentic finance demo. Reusing on-chain Paid events as a credit signal is a clean, unfakeable data source no off-chain underwriter has.

Where to improve: (1) the testnet RPC caps eth_getLogs to a 10k-block range with no pagination cursor — reading an address's full history needs manual chunking; a richer logs/indexer endpoint would help agents that reason over history. (2) Native-USDC-as-gas with 18-decimal wei is elegant but surprising (USDC is usually 6-decimal) — a docs note on the decimals model would prevent confusion. (3) More worked "agentic finance on Arc" examples (escrow, factoring, payroll) would shortcut a lot of first-principles contract work.
